Before you come in

What a transfer usually needs.

It depends on how the car changed hands. This covers most private party sales. Call us if yours is unusual and we'll tell you what applies.

Inherited a vehicle, or the owner has passed away? That is a different set of forms and we handle those too. Call us before you gather anything.

  • The title, signed by the seller in the right place
  • A bill of sale with the price and date
  • The odometer reading at the time of sale
  • A smog certificate, usually the seller's responsibility
  • Your photo ID and proof of insurance
  • Lien release if the car was ever financed
Common questions

Questions we get every day.

The seller signed in the wrong box.
It happens constantly. Depending on where they signed, it can sometimes be corrected with a statement of facts form, and sometimes the seller has to sign something new. Bring it in and we'll tell you which.
I lost the title.
The registered owner can apply for a duplicate. If you're the buyer and the seller lost it, the seller has to request it, so don't hand over money until that's sorted.
Do I have to transfer it right away?
California gives a buyer 10 days to submit the transfer. After that, penalties start adding up, so it's worth doing quickly even if the car is going to sit.
Was it a gift or a family transfer?
Family transfers between parents, children, spouses, and some other relationships can be exempt from a smog requirement and use a different form. Tell us the relationship and we'll apply the right one.
Can you do it while I wait?
Yes, as long as the paperwork is complete. That is the whole reason to come here instead of mailing it in and hoping.
Good to know

Where title transfers usually go wrong

California gives a buyer 10 days to file after a sale, and penalties start after that. Most people who get it wrong get it wrong once and repeat the trip.

A standard private party title transfer needs the title signed by the registered owner in the right place, the odometer reading, a bill of sale, and usually a smog certificate. If the car was ever financed, the lien has to be released first.

The most common problem we see is a signature in the wrong box. Sometimes that is fixable with a statement of facts and sometimes the seller has to sign again. We can tell you which at the counter. Gifts and family transfers follow a different path with different forms, so mention the relationship.

Inherited vehicles are their own category, and the route depends on whether the estate went through probate. Call before gathering paperwork for one of those, because the order you do things in matters more than usual.

One note for sellers rather than buyers: California expects a notice of transfer and release of liability after a sale. It protects you from citations the new owner collects before they file. People skip it constantly and hear about it months later.
