The plan / 07

Three plays, in the order they pay.

01

Search — build the pages that already almost rank

Five pages first, for the searches where you’re already close off the homepage alone: bad credit, no credit, buy here pay here, first-time buyer, trade-in. You’re at #6 and #7 for two of them with no page at all — that’s about as close to a sure thing as this work gets. Then the Auto City move: reviews and comparisons of the cars you actually stock, so the site stops shrinking every time inventory turns over.

02

Search — fix the two things quietly costing you

Your website says (619) 350-2259. Your Google profile says (619) 230-5075. Google reads consistency across listings as a signal for who to show on the map, and right now you’re giving it two answers. Second: you’re an LLC with “Rental” in the name, you have a car-rental category on your Google profile, and the word “rental” appears nowhere on your website. That’s a second revenue line with no front door.

03

Email — harvest the list you already have

Three sequences. A text after every delivery asking for the review, which is what turns 55 into 200 and makes the eight one-stars stop mattering. Everybody who filled out pre-approval and didn’t buy — the most qualified list you own, currently going nowhere. And the twelve-month check-in: somebody who’s made a year of on-time payments has moved up a credit tier and can refinance or trade up, and you’re the dealer who said yes when nobody else would. Nobody in this market runs that.

04

Google Ads — buy the cheap clicks, not the expensive ones

Everyone fights over “buy here pay here san diego” and bids it to $14.63 for 110 searches a month. “Used cars san diego” is 3,600 searches at $2.91. Same customer, a third of the click price, twenty times the pool — you do the qualifying in the ad copy and on the landing page instead of in the bid. One exception worth taking: “first time car buyer program” is high intent and genuinely underbid at competition 43 of 100.

05

Yelp — reviews first, then ads

Yelp’s own “Top 10 Bad Credit Used Car Sales in San Diego” list ranks #8 on Google for your best search, and you’re not on it. But with six reviews and a 3.5, paying to send traffic to that profile is paying to show people your weakest page. Reviews first. Then Yelp ads put you inside your competitors’ profiles, which is the cheapest conquest buy in this market.
