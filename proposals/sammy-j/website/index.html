What goes on it / 04

What one of those pages actually says.

Same shape every time. Using the bad-credit page as the example:

01

A headline that matches what they typed

“Bad credit car loans in San Diego” — not “About Sammy J Auto Sales”. This one line is the biggest difference between you and the six dealers above you.

02

The promise, then the proof

You get approved where the bank said no — then straight away your star rating and how many happy customers you have. Say it, then back it up.

03

“What you need to bring”

Licence, proof of income, how much down, what happens if they have had a car taken back. This is the question everybody has and nearly no dealer answers online. It is the block that makes people phone you instead of leaving.

04

A real example of a payment

What a typical approval looks like — money down, monthly, how long. Vague financing talk is why most of these pages do nothing.

05

Your cars, pulled in automatically

The page stays put forever. The cars inside it change as you sell them. That is the difference that keeps you on Google.

And at the bottom, answers to these

Your 4.3 is not a quality problem. You just have too few reviews.

Of your 55 reviews, 44 are five stars. Eight are one star. With a total that small, those eight drag the whole average down. Get ten to fifteen new ones a month and they stop mattering inside three months. You do not need to chase the bad ones. You need more of the good ones.

Yelp, in this order.

01

Reviews first, before you spend anything

You have six reviews on Yelp and a 3.5. Paying Yelp to send people there today is paying to show people your worst page. The texts above fix this in about three months.

02

Fill the page in properly

Photos, hours, the financing detail, and the rentals. Yelp’s own “best bad credit dealers in San Diego” list is the 8th thing Google shows for your main search — and you are not on it.

03

Then pay

Yelp ads put you on your competitors’ pages. Once the reviews are fixed it is the cheapest way to get in front of someone already shopping. Not before.

Recap / 07

What you get.

The build — once

  • Six new pages: bad credit, weekly payments, first-time buyers, rentals, and three for your vehicle types
  • Two pages redone: financing and trade-in
  • Front page headline fixed
  • Phone number made the same everywhere
  • Answers to the questions Google is already asking
  • Forms that actually go somewhere

Google — every month

  • Four write-ups a month about the cars you actually stock
  • Comparisons, reviews, straight answers — the Auto City play
  • Your Google listing kept up to date
  • A monthly report showing where you sit against the lots in this document

Texts and emails — every month

  • Review request after every sale
  • Follow-up for people who applied and did not buy
  • The one-year check-in and trade-up offer
  • Alerts when a car someone asked for arrives

Ads — every month

  • Google ads on the cheap, high-volume searches
  • Blocking the searches that waste money
  • Yelp ads once reviews pass 30
  • Call tracking, so you can see which ads made the phone ring

Pages first.

They are the cheapest thing on this list, they are the only part that keeps paying you back year after year, and the other two both need them to exist before they are worth running.
