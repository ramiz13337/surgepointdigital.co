The build plan.

What gets built, in what order, and what each piece is for. The pages ship first, on their own. Email needs somewhere to link and ads need somewhere to land — right now there is nowhere to send anybody except a homepage that opens with the word “About.”

The order / 01

Pages first. Email and ads after.

PlanningNow

Why this order: you are already #6 and #7 for “bad credit auto dealers san diego” and “san diego car dealerships bad credit” with no page for either. Building those pages is the cheapest win on the table, and it is the thing the other two programs depend on. After launch each program is a fixed scope you decide on separately — nothing here is a bundle you have to take whole.

Page template / 03

What each of those pages looks like.

Same skeleton every time, so they are quick to build and quick to add to. Using the bad-credit page as the example:

01

A headline that matches the search

“Bad credit car loans in San Diego” — not “About Sammy J Auto Sales”. This single line is the biggest gap between you and the six dealers above you.

02

The promise, then the proof

Approved where the bank said no, followed immediately by the 4.3 on Google and the count of five-star customers. Claim, then evidence, in that order.

03

“What you need to bring”

The actual question every subprime buyer has and almost nobody answers on a website: licence, proof of income, down payment, what happens if there is a repo on file. This block is why people call instead of bouncing.

04

A real payment example

What a typical approval looks like — down, monthly, term. Vague financing copy is why these pages normally fail.

05

Live inventory strip

Pulled from the feed you already have, so it stays current. The page is permanent; the cars inside it rotate. That is the difference that keeps the ranking.

06

Apply form and phone, twice

Once above the fold, once at the bottom. Both go to a real inbox and a real sequence rather than a form that disappears.

07

Paid / 06

Buy the cheap clicks. Fix Yelp before paying Yelp.

Google Ads — three campaigns
01

Local inventory — the volume

“Used cars san diego” is 3,600 searches a month at $2.91. “Cars for sale san diego” is 1,300 at $2.44. About 5,870 searches at roughly $2.85 blended.

02

Subprime intent — the underbid one

Everyone bids “buy here pay here san diego” to $14.63 for 110 searches. Skip it. “First time car buyer program” is high intent at competition 43 of 100 — the one genuinely cheap term in the category.

03

Brand defence

Cheap, and it stops other dealers buying your name. You already rank #1 for it organically; this protects the click.

The whole trick: do the credit qualifying in the ad copy and on the landing page, not in the bid. Same customer, a third of the click price, twenty times the pool.

Yelp — in this order
01

Reviews first

Six reviews and a 3.5. Paying to send traffic there today is paying to show people your weakest page. The delivery sequence above fixes this in a quarter.

02

Complete the profile

Photos, hours, the financing detail, and the rental line that exists nowhere online. Yelp’s own “Top 10 Bad Credit Used Car Sales in San Diego” list ranks #8 on Google for your best search and you are not on it.

03

Then ads

Yelp ads place you inside competitors’ profiles. With the review count fixed, it is the cheapest conquest buy in this market. Not before.

Recap / 07

What’s included.

Build — one time

  • Six new pages: bad credit, buy here pay here, no credit / first-time buyer, rentals, and three inventory categories
  • Two rewrites: financing and trade-in
  • Homepage heading and title fixed
  • Phone number reconciled across the site, Google and Yelp
  • FAQ blocks built from Google’s own People Also Ask
  • Forms wired to a real inbox and the sequences below

SEO — monthly

  • Four articles a month on the cars you actually stock
  • Comparisons, model reviews, maintenance answers
  • Google Business Profile upkeep and posts
  • Rank and traffic reporting against the dealers in the audit

Email / SMS — monthly

  • Delivery → review sequence (Google, then Yelp)
  • Pre-approval save sequence
  • 12-month credit check-in and trade-up
  • Car Finder match alerts

Paid — monthly

  • Google Ads: local inventory, subprime intent, brand defence
  • Negative keyword list and search term review
  • Yelp Ads once reviews clear 30
  • Call tracking so you can see what each campaign actually produced

Pages first. They are the cheapest thing on this list, they are the only part that compounds, and both other programs need them to exist before they are worth running. Everything after launch is scoped and decided on separately.
