Structure / 03

What's on the site: 11 pages.

Google ranks pages, not businesses. Someone searching "title transfer san diego" needs to land on a title transfer page, not a homepage that mentions it somewhere. So every service gets its own page. Renewals is the one that takes payment online. Title transfers and VIN verification are office visits, so those pages sell the trip in, not a checkout. Dashed boxes get added later, month by month.

Getting Stripe into the spreadsheet
three ways · the checkout is identical, they only differ in how the row shows up
Recommended 01 · Webhook Stripe → Apps Script

A short script lives inside the sheet itself. Stripe posts every completed payment to it and the row appears the second the card clears. No third party in the middle, nothing to keep paying for.

free
set up once, about an hour
02 · No code connector Make or Zapier

A connector watches Stripe and writes the row for you. Same result as the webhook, set up by clicking through a screen instead of pasting a script.

Make: free to ~1,000 orders/mo
Zapier: ~$20/mo
03 · No integration at all Stripe export

Stripe already logs every payment with search and filters built in. Export to a spreadsheet whenever you want to run a campaign. Worth knowing this is a real option.

free
manual, fine under ~20 orders/mo
Where the plate and phone number come from.

Stripe's own checkout can collect a couple of extra fields on top of the card, so the plate and phone get captured at payment rather than in a separate form. Those come through on the same webhook as the payment. One link, one screen, one row. If it turns out we need more fields than Stripe allows, a hosted form in front of Stripe handles it and the rest of the chain is unchanged.

The sheet is the starting point, not the ceiling.

Start here because there is nothing to build and nothing to learn. If you would rather orders land in the CRM you already run, the webhook points there instead, or at both, and nothing else changes. Either way every row carries a plate, a service and a date, which is all next year's renewal reminders need.

After launch / 07

What the monthly SEO program does.

Tags Clinic went from ranking for nothing to 5,634 keywords in 18 months doing exactly this, every month, without stopping. Three parts.

Part 1 · Growing your market share
new pages every month, each aimed at a search with a customer behind it
01
The forms library

A page for every DMV form people Google in a panic: REG 5, REG 101, REG 156 and dozens more. This exact play built Tags Clinic's entire lead. Every one published is another door into your shop.

02
Panic pages

Expired tags, late fees, registration holds, failed smog. People search these at 11pm, stressed, ready to pay whoever answers first. That gets to be you.

03
En Español

Full Spanish versions of the money pages. Huge demand in this county and almost nobody serves it properly. Near zero competition for entire neighborhoods of customers.

04
Neighborhood pages

Mira Mesa, Sorrento Valley, Scripps Ranch, Kearny Mesa, UTC. Every "dmv services near me" within ten minutes of the shop becomes your search result.

Part 2 · Getting Google to trust you
the signals, outside your site, that decide who shows up on the map
05
Listings that all match

Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing, BBB and the local directories, all claimed, all saying the same name, address and phone. Mismatched listings quietly kill map rankings. Yours get made airtight.

06
Local links

Dealer partners, local business groups and local press pointing at your site. Every link is a vote that you're the real one on Miramar Rd.

07
An active Google profile

Posts, photos and answered questions every month. Google shows busy profiles above dormant ones, and most shops let theirs go quiet.
